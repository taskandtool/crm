# The voice

Whoever writes a web page, an ad, a post or an email reads `brand/voice.md`
first. Back every line in it with one of the owner's own
sentences, quoted: a writer can't copy a voice from adjectives. Leave a
line with no sample "to fill".

## What to collect

Rank samples by how unpolished they are, best first:

1. The owner talking or writing to someone: chat, emails, call transcripts,
   replies to reviews.
2. Their own social posts.
3. Their website and brochures, which an agency may have written.

Customers' reviews aren't the business's voice. Their phrases still go in
the lexicon, because they show how buyers describe the problem.

## What to write

- **The card.** Who reads, who speaks, and the relationship between them
  (peer, guide, operator, expert, host). Then the register, cadence,
  vocabulary, how they prove things (demonstration, numbers, examples,
  process, credentials), humour, taboos and the main action.
- **Three sentences** the business could say, written once the card is
  filled. Show them to the owner and ask "Does this sound like you?"
- **Signatures.** Three to five moves people would recognise, each with the
  sentence that shows it and where it came from.
- **Never.** Words and moves the business doesn't use, each with the reason.
- **Rewrites.** Three to five pairs: the generic version, then the same
  thing in this voice.
- **Lexicon.** Their names for things, spelt their way. Customer phrases,
  word for word. Words the owner uses that a marketer wouldn't.
- **How the sentences run,** measured on the samples: average length, the
  shortest and longest, how they open, contractions, exclamation marks.
- **Tone by surface.** What changes, and what never changes, on a web page,
  an ad, a social post and an email.
- **Sources.** The raw files the samples came from.

## Example

A signature, with its evidence:

> **Tells you when not to spend.** "A £20 part will fix that.
> Don't let anyone sell you a new boiler." (the owner's reply to a Google review,
> 2026-03-04, from `raw/places/`)

A rewrite pair:

> Generic: "We offer comprehensive boiler servicing at competitive prices." <!-- tropes-allow -->
> In this voice: "A service is £85. If it needs a part, I'll tell you what
> it costs before I fit it."
