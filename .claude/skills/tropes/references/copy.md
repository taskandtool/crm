# Copy tells

Structures outlast word lists: the vocabulary moves with each model generation, the shapes do not. `tropes.mjs` finds the ones a pattern can (the id beside a trope is the rule it prints; the phrase tables are `refused-phrase`); the rest need an ear.

## Today's models (2025 to 2026)

The older lists below are mostly 2023's, and people wrote many of them before any model did ("seamlessly", "all-in-one" and "say goodbye to" are all on 2020 homepages). These are what current Claude and GPT models write now, from Wikipedia's *Signs of AI writing* and the claudisms.ai catalog, each checked against pages people wrote before 2022:

| Trope | Examples | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Figures of speech `model-tell` | "lands in your inbox", "the report lands every Monday", "replies land in Messages", "where it lands", "surfaces the issues", "hands you a brief", "stays yours", "load-bearing", "That matters.", "because it matters", "worth a look", "at its core", "this is where X comes in", "lean into", "trust compounds"; hints: quietly, genuinely, honestly, "lives in one place", "less X, more Y" | The literal verb: appears, arrives, starts in, shows, gives. Or cut the line |
| Staged reveal `model-tell` | "Here's the real issue:", "Here's the kicker:", "The real problem is…", "That's the whole point." | Say the point |
| "X, not Y" `negation-tail` | "Fill your chairs, not your phone", "progress every week, not just at the end" | State X. Keep a "not" a customer needs: "Cash only, not cards." |
| Pivot `negation-pivot` | "It's not a builder, it's a growth engine", "This isn't about software. It's about your time.", "Not a template. A real site.", "is more than a CRM, it's…" | State Y |
| "With" rider `with-rider` | "Your site runs on Cloudflare, with its SEO and design done professionally." | End at the comma; say the rest with its own subject |
| Even rhythm (by ear) | every lede one clause long, every line starting "We…" or with the company's name | Vary who acts and how long the sentences run |
| Comma tails (by ear) | "Yards that look looked-after, all season", "built for salons and barbershops" after a comma | Common in people's copy too, so it never fails: rewrite the ones that read as an afterthought |

## Today's sentence shapes

Found by reading model-written pages line by line. The script catches two (`order-result`, `effort-once`); the rest need the cold read. For every line ask: would a person say this out loud, and does the reader care?

| Trope | Examples | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Order joined to a vague result `order-result` | "Send us a price list and the site uses it", "Save the brand once and every page uses it" | Who does what, in order, with the real verb: "Send us your price list and we update the prices page." |
| A vague verb for the real action | "the site uses it", "handles your bookings", "takes care of the rest", "powers your store" | The literal action: "puts the prices on your prices page" |
| An unnamed actor | "Send it your details" (who is it?), "is updated" with nobody updating, "all of this" a skimmer can't trace back | Name who or what it is |
| Insisting it's real | "real people answer the phone", "a site that actually works", "genuine local experts" | Cut the adjective; nobody raised the doubt |
| Work as the actor | "Routine maintenance runs on a schedule", "the work runs by itself", "Your lawn stays green all summer" | Say who does it: "We service the boiler every October", "Our crew mows every Tuesday" |
| No-effort "once" `effort-once` | "Ask once, and we handle the rest", "Set it once", "Book once and you're covered" | Say the step: "book the first visit and we schedule the rest". "Once a week" is fine |
| Control closers | "when you ask", "whenever you ask", "when you say so", "until you approve it", "before it does it", "It never can." | Cut. Say once, where the reader would worry, who decides |
| Safety-net closers | "…and nothing is lost", "…and you're never left without cover" added after every limit or failure | Say it once, where the limit is explained |
| Tacked-on feeling | "while you sleep", "while you're away", "so you can focus on what matters" | Cut the ending; the sentence already said when or what |
| Familiarity frames | "the list you keep today", "the accounts your business already uses", "the tools you already love", "however you invoice today" | Name the thing: "your HubSpot export", "Stripe and QuickBooks" |
| "One" as the selling point | "in one place", "one dashboard for everything" | Say what the reader can do there: "See every client's bookings and bills" |
| Ownership and limits as a refrain | "your own Stripe", "each X gets its own Y", "only" and "never" in every section | "Own" only where the reader fears sharing; say a permission once, as what a person can do |
| Slogan headings | "Everyone starts the day knowing what's due", "Your kitchen, your rules", "Peace of mind, one visit at a time" | A plain fact or a plain label |
| A closer that echoes the button | the heading "Book your first visit" over a "Book your first visit" button | The last heading says one more thing about the page |
| Table stakes as features | "easy online booking", "friendly staff", "HTTPS included", "cancel whenever you like" | Cut, unless the reader would doubt it |
| Process where the result belongs | a hero that walks through call, quote, schedule | Lead with what the reader gets; the process is a later sentence |
| Circular or empty lines | "solutions that solve your problems", "services that serve you", "results that matter" | Say what it produces |
| Lists and chains in one breath | two lists of three in a two-sentence hero; "Builds…, adds…, and keeps…" on every card | One list a section; give other lines one subject and one verb |

## Older vocabulary

| Trope | Examples | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Verb cosplay | leverage, harness, unlock, unleash, empower, elevate, transform, revolutionize, supercharge, streamline, foster, navigate, embark | The plain verb for the actual action: use, open, cut, book, fit |
| Inflated adjectives | seamless, robust, cutting-edge, world-class, game-changing, comprehensive, holistic, innovative, bespoke, curated, next-level, all-in-one | A number, a material, a limit: "fitted in 3 days", "solid oak doors" |
| Significance inflation | stands as a testament, plays a pivotal/crucial role, underscores, evolving landscape, setting the stage, indelible mark | Say what happened and to whom, or cut it |
| Brochure puffery | nestled, in the heart of, boasts, vibrant, rich heritage, renowned, diverse array | Say where it is and what it has |
| Metaphor nouns | tapestry, realm, landscape, ecosystem, journey, solutions | Name the thing the business sells |
| Era vocabulary `dated-vocabulary` | delve, intricate, meticulous, showcasing, highlighting, fostering, enhance, align with | Density counts, one word doesn't |
| Boosters with no anchor | significantly, remarkably, incredibly, truly | The figure, or nothing |
| Small-business clichés | We're passionate about, We pride ourselves, We do things differently, Your partner in X, Making X simple | A fact only this business can say |

## Structures

| Trope | Examples | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Negation pivot** (strongest tell) `negation-pivot`, `negation-tail` | "It's not X, it's Y", "not just X but Y", "Not because X. Because Y.", "X, not Y."; "Y rather than X" by ear (people write it often) | State Y. Nobody proposed X |
| Rule of three `triads` (staccato runs fail; two lists in the hero are a hint; the rest by ear) | three adjectives, three bullets, three examples per section; a chain of commands ("Call us, get a quote, and book a time") | Use the number of things there really are. At most one list of three a section, and never the same three-part shape on every card |
| Staccato triplet `triads` | "No fluff. No filler. No stress." "Fast. Simple. Effective." | One plain sentence with the real claim |
| Throat-clearing openers | In today's fast-paced world; In a world where; Imagine a world; Welcome to; Are you looking for; When it comes to | Start with the offer |
| Audience sweep | Whether you're X or Y; Look no further; something for everyone | Name the one reader |
| Fake-casual reveal | Here's the thing; And honestly?; You know what's wild?; But here's what nobody's saying; That changes everything | Delete it. The next sentence is the content |
| Stock question then answer `rhetorical-question` | "Tired of X? We've got you.", "Looking for X? Look no further." | Say what you offer. A hook's real question with a specific answer is fine ("Radiators cold at the top? That's not the boiler.") |
| "-ing" rider `ing-rider` | ", highlighting / ensuring / fostering / showcasing / reflecting / contributing to…" | Cut the clause, or make it a sentence with a subject |
| Copula avoidance | serves as, stands as, functions as, features, offers, boasts | is, has |
| Hedges and filler | It's worth noting; It's important to note; It goes without saying; Some experts argue | Cut. If it's uncertain, say what is uncertain |
| Fake balance | "While X has merits, Y offers benefits" | Pick one and say why |
| Summary closers | In conclusion; Ultimately; Remember, …; In summary | Stop at the last fact or the call to action |
| Fit with no facts `vague-fit` | shaped to your work, built around you, your way, tailored to you, for how you work, the work your team really does, built for scale | Name what changes to fit: "your stages and fields", "open till 9 on Thursdays" |
| Formula headlines | X, reimagined; X, redefined; Where X meets Y; Built for the future; Everything you need, all in one place | A headline a stranger could repeat: what the reader can do here |
| Echoes | headline restates the company's own tagline/brief; a customer's quote used as a heading | Rewrite from the reader's question; quotes go in quotation marks with a name |
| Joined heading `joined-heading` | "Fall cleanup in Larkfield, from $90"; "What we do, and what it costs"; "Garden design: a plan in a week, planted in spring" | One plain sentence a stranger could repeat, or a plain label: "Fall cleanup in Larkfield starts at $90", "What each job costs". A list's commas are fine |
| Restated heading `restates-heading` | the line under "Fast boiler repairs in Leeds" is "We do fast boiler repairs across Leeds" | The line adds the fact the heading could not hold: the price, the area, the wait |
| Repeated phrase `repeated-phrase` (a hint) | the same four-word phrase in two sections of a page or ad | Say it once, where it matters most. The call to action is the exception: one wording everywhere |
| Weak call to action `weak-cta` (a hint) | Learn more, Click here, Submit, Read more | Name what the reader gets: "Book a survey", "Check availability". "Get started" is fine for a product people sign up to |
| Things doing what people do | "the cabin heats itself", passive hiding the actor | Name the person who does it |

## Rhythm and formatting

| Trope | Fix |
|---|---|
| Long heading `long-heading` (a hint): a heading over 12 words | One thing per heading; the line under it says the rest |
| Uniform sentence length (by ear): every sentence about as long as the last | Over six or more sentences, the longest is at least 12 words longer than the shortest |
| Low punctuation variety: long "and"-joined sentences, few commas/semicolons, no parentheses | Break at the "and". Let one sentence carry an aside |
| Paragraph uniformity (claim, example, restatement, repeated) | Vary the order. Some paragraphs are one line |
| Em dashes `em-dash` doing a comma's or full stop's job (house rule; weak signal) | Comma, colon, or new sentence |
| Bold list stems, title-case headings, emoji bullets, a heading every 80 words, markdown bleed (`**`, `#`) | Sentence case. Bold only what a skimmer must see |
| "Broetry" (one line a paragraph building to a cliché) on LinkedIn | Two or three sentence paragraphs, varied |
| Same sentence pattern in every heading | Mix labels and claims; a heading that is a claim must be checkable |
| Same opening again and again: "Our team…" on five headings, "Builds… / Tracks… / Writes…" on every card, "If you…" leading lede after lede | Vary who or what starts the line |
| The same closing frame on every page ("Ready to get started?", "Book your X today.") | Each page's last heading says something only that page can |
| The same phrase in headings across a site ("…by asking AI" on every page, "…in minutes" on every card) | Keep it on one page, usually the homepage; elsewhere say what that page's reader does |
| "It also…" tacked on to finish a lede | Make it its own sentence with its own point, or cut it |
| Brand ego `we-over-you`: we/our outnumbers you/your (an error in an ad, a hint on a page, where an about section may say we) | At least parity |

## Claims

| Trope | Fix |
|---|---|
| Unsupported superlatives ("the best", "#1", "trusted by thousands") | Cut unless a source with a date says so |
| Puffery (by ear): certified, honest, quality, trusted, premier, top-rated, reliable, professional, leading, with no number beside them | The certificate and its number, the review count and where, the years; or cut |
| Invented proof: stats, studies, testimonials, review counts, "a customer", hardcoded five stars | Only from the owner's material, with name, count, source, date. If there is no source, it becomes a `NEED:` question for the owner. Never soften it into a vaguer claim |
| Vague attribution ("industry reports", "experts agree", "studies show") | Name the source or cut |
| Generic examples / cookie-cutter case studies | One real job, place, number or name per section |
| Fake urgency ("only 3 left", countdowns) | Only when true and sourced |
| Personal-attribute phrasing in ads `personal-attribute` ("Struggling with your debt?", "Are you overweight?") | Meta refuses it. Speak about the offer, not the reader's condition |
| A number as the whole answer ("15 towns") | The list goes with the number |
