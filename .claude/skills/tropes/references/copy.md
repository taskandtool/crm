# Copy tells

What Claude Opus 5.5 and GPT-6.1 Sol write that people rarely do, drawn
from their own drafts (November 2026). Read the copy twice: once for who
does each verb, then for everything else. A rule name in brackets means the
script also flags some of it.

## Pass 1: who does the verb

Go sentence by sentence and ask who does the verb. Today's models make
things act: a product, the site, a page, a form, a plan, a price, a
schedule, alerts, a problem. People don't talk that way. Rewrite each one
the way the owner would say it: "we" when the business does the work, "you"
when the reader does or gets something.

Draft: "The site emails your team when someone books." <!-- tropes-allow -->
Rewrite: "You can get an email when someone books."
Draft: "Pingwell checks your site every 30 seconds." <!-- tropes-allow -->
Rewrite: "We check your site every 30 seconds."
Draft: "Our quarterly plans keep your home protected all year." <!-- tropes-allow -->
Rewrite: "We treat your home every three months."
Draft: "Electrical problems don't wait for business hours." <!-- tropes-allow -->
Rewrite: "Call us at any hour."
Draft: "A short blip gets caught." "Status pages that update themselves." <!-- tropes-allow -->
Rewrite: "We catch short blips too." "We update your status page after every check."

People are the reader, the owner, the staff, a named person, and the
business speaking as "we" or by its name. "Is", "has" and "costs" are fine
("Plans cost $49 a month"). An animal or event doing what it really does
is fine ("termites eat wood"); doing what only a person does is not ("pests
don't take a season off"). The order-and-result shape is the same tell
[order-result]: "Send us a price list and the site uses it." <!-- tropes-allow -->

## Pass 2: the other tells, worst first

**1. The pivot** [negation-pivot, negation-tail]. Nobody suggested the
other thing: "It's not a website builder. It's a growth engine." "There's <!-- tropes-allow -->
no lecture, just good care." "people who know your yard, not a call
centre." Say the claim. A "not" the reader needs stays: "Cash only, not cards."

**2. Scenes made up for colour.** A vivid time, person or moment nobody
supplied: "3am on a January Sunday", "from a toddler's first visit to
Grandpa's crown", "a quiet spot for morning coffee". Use a real job,
customer or place from the notes, or the plain fact.

**3. Reassurance nobody asked for.** "We show up when we say we will", "a
real person will answer", "no surprises", "nothing is lost", "until you
approve it". The no-effort promise is the same [effort-once]: "Set it once
and forget it." Say a limit or a permission once, where the reader would worry.

**4. Rhythm by formula** [triads]. Headings of clipped pairs: "Local shop.
Experienced hands." "Build the schedule. Keep service moving." Origins as a
count: "Two sisters, one shovel and a borrowed truck." "No fluff. No filler.
Just results." Use the real number of things; one list of three per section at most.

**5. The comma afterthought** [with-rider]. A second idea after a comma,
often "with", an "-ing" word or "so": "Ledgerly sorts them into categories, <!-- tropes-allow -->
helping you stay organized." Give each idea its own sentence and subject, or cut it.

**6. Figures of speech** [model-tell]. "The report lands every Monday", "it
hands you a brief", "trust compounds", "this is where X comes in", "Here's
the kicker:". Use the literal verb or say the point.

**7. Fit and names with no facts** [vague-fit]. "Built around how you
work", "tailored to your needs", "in one place", "the tools you already
use". Name the thing: "Your stages, your fields, your price list."

**8. Refrains.** The same word or shape in every section: "every" and "each"
in most sentences; "only", "never" and "its own" as a chorus; four headings
that start the same way; every bullet a "**Bold term.** Verb…". Vary who
starts the line and say a limit once.

**9. Invented proof.** Stats, review counts, ratings, quotes, history,
"trusted by thousands". These come only from the owner's notes, with the
count, source and date. Without one, write a `NEED:` line. Never soften it
into a vaguer claim.

**10. Placeholders left in.** "[phone]", "[Item 1]", "2025-XX-XX". Today's
models leave these where a fact is missing; find it or ask with a `NEED:` line.

**11. Puffery and table stakes.** "Quality", "trusted", "professional", or
"easy online booking" and "friendly staff" offered as features. Put the
evidence beside it (licence number, review count and where, years) or cut it.

**12. Ads about the reader's condition** [personal-attribute]. "Struggling
with debt?" Meta rejects these. Talk about the offer.

## Headings and layout

- A heading with a tail after a comma: "Saturdays, because life is busy", <!-- tropes-allow -->
  "Insurance, made simple", "Your transactions, imported automatically",
  "Breakdown callouts, day or night". Write one plain sentence or a plain
  label. A place, a date or a real list is fine ("Furnace repair in
  Austin, TX", "Time tracking, invoicing and accounting").
- A count is not a heading. Say what the things do: "Start from a website,
  CRM or Work Board" over "Six apps to start from". A number with no list
  ("15 towns") gets its list.
- No em dashes [em-dash]. Use a comma, a colon or a new sentence.
- Sentence case. Bold only what a skimmer must see; no emoji bullets, and no
  `**` or `#` showing on the page. The last heading says something only that
  page could.

## Articles and longer prose

- Answer in the first paragraph. No opening about why the topic matters, no
  summary or one-line moral at the end.
- No discovery arc ("the thing I keep coming back to", "here's where I
  landed") and no judging ("the most interesting part"). Show the detail.
- Vary sentence length; break long "and" chains; put an aside in one sentence.
- Name sources ("studies show" is a tell) and use one real job, place,
  number or name per section.
